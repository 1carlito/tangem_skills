# Source Strategy

Design the search around the decision and evidence needed, not around collecting links.

## Query decomposition

Create sub-questions for:

- Definitions and baseline facts
- Current state and trend
- Option-specific outcomes
- Costs, constraints, and implementation
- Risks and failure cases
- Stakeholder or customer evidence
- Regulatory or jurisdictional differences
- Counterarguments and disconfirming evidence
- Precedents and comparable decisions

For each sub-question, specify:

- Preferred source type
- Query terms and synonyms
- Geography, language, and date range
- Inclusion and exclusion criteria
- Stop condition

## Source hierarchy

Choose the strongest available source for the claim:

1. Primary law, regulation, standard, filing, dataset, documentation, or original research
2. Independent analysis with transparent data and methodology
3. Reputable reporting that cites named evidence
4. Trade publications, expert commentary, and institutional blogs
5. Forums, social posts, reviews, and anonymous accounts

Lower tiers can reveal hypotheses, experiences, or emerging issues. They should not carry a
consequential factual claim when stronger evidence is available.

## Source independence

Several pages repeating the same press release count as one evidence chain.

Check:

- Shared underlying dataset
- Syndicated article or wire copy
- Citations back to one original source
- Common funding, ownership, or commercial interest
- Multiple analyses by the same author or institution

Record the dependency in the ledger and avoid inflated corroboration.

## Primary-source retrieval

When a secondary source cites a report, study, filing, or official statement:

1. Retrieve the original.
2. Verify that it supports the secondary source's wording.
3. Cite the original for the factual claim.
4. Cite the secondary analysis only for its distinct interpretation.

## Time and scope

Record:

- Publication and data-collection dates
- Forecast horizon
- Geography and jurisdiction
- Population or sample
- Product/version context
- Definitions and units

Do not combine evidence with incompatible scopes without explaining the difference.

## Contradiction search

For every recommendation-critical claim, search deliberately for:

- Failed replications
- Contrary datasets
- Different jurisdictions or segments
- Methodological criticism
- Later corrections or retractions
- Incentives and conflicts of interest
- Evidence favoring another option

Describe whether disagreement comes from facts, definitions, methods, values, or forecasts.

## Stop conditions

Stop searching a sub-question when:

- The required primary source and an independent corroborating source are verified
- Additional results repeat the same evidence chain
- The decision would not change with more detail
- The source limit or deadline is reached
- Remaining uncertainty is better resolved by an experiment or internal data

Do not claim exhaustiveness unless the corpus and search method genuinely support it.

## Freshness

Prefer current sources when facts change over time. Retain older sources when they:

- Establish history or original methodology
- Remain the governing authority
- Provide a useful baseline
- Are necessary to explain a trend

Never label a source stale based on age alone; judge whether the underlying fact is time-sensitive.
