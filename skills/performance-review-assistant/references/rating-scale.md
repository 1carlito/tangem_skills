# Performance Rating Scale

Five-point rating system with calibration guidance.

## 1 – Below Expectations / Does Not Meet Standards

- Consistently misses key responsibilities
- Performance significantly below job requirements
- Requires immediate improvement or performance plan
- **Frequency**: 0–5% of workforce
- **Action**: Initiate PIP; involve HR

## 2 – Partially Meets Expectations / Needs Improvement

- Meets some but not all key responsibilities
- Performance below expected level for tenure
- Needs development plan and support
- Common for new employees ramping or those with emerging issues
- **Frequency**: 5–15% of workforce
- **Action**: Create development plan with clear milestones

## 3 – Meets Expectations / Fully Proficient

- Consistently meets core job requirements
- Performs at expected level for tenure
- Solid contributor with no developmental concerns
- Standard performance for most roles
- **Frequency**: 60–75% of workforce (healthy organization)
- **Action**: Continue support; identify stretch opportunities

## 4 – Exceeds Expectations / High Performer

- Significantly exceeds core job requirements
- Delivers measurable value beyond role scope
- Takes on stretch projects and mentors others
- High potential for advancement
- **Frequency**: 10–20% of workforce
- **Action**: Invest in accelerated development; discuss career path

## 5 – Far Exceeds Expectations / Outstanding Performer

- Exceptional performance across all dimensions
- Rare combination of skills and impact
- Potential for executive or expert leadership roles
- Drives significant organizational success
- **Frequency**: 1–5% of workforce
- **Action**: Retention-critical; accelerate career opportunities

## Calibration guidance

### Distribution health check

| Rating | Healthy range | Red flags |
|--------|---------------|-----------|
| 1–2    | 5–15%         | 0% may indicate avoidance of hard conversations |
| 3      | 60–75%        | <50% suggests inflation; >85% suggests underinvestment in top performers |
| 4–5    | 15–25%        | >30% indicates rating inflation |

### Avoiding common biases

- **Recency bias**: Review full performance period, not just last month
- **Halo effect**: Separate dimensions; one strength does not elevate all ratings
- **Leniency bias**: Compare against job expectations, not other employees
- **Similarity bias**: Evaluate objectively regardless of personal rapport
- **Central tendency**: Use full scale; "3 for everyone" is not meaningful

### Cross-manager calibration

Before finalizing ratings:

1. Managers share proposed ratings with peer managers
2. Discuss outliers (1s, 2s, and 5s) with specific examples
3. Ensure consistent interpretation of "meets expectations" across teams
4. HR facilitates calibration sessions for large organizations
